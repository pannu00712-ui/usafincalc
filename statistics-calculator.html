What This Statistics Calculator Does

Enter a data set and get a full descriptive statistics summary in one place — mean, median, mode, range, variance, standard deviation, and more — instead of calculating each measure separately.

How It Works

This calculator runs your data set through the standard descriptive statistics formulas: the mean (sum divided by count), median (middle value when sorted), mode (most frequent value), range (max minus min), variance (average squared deviation from the mean), and standard deviation (the square root of variance).

Why Look at Multiple Measures Together

No single statistic tells the whole story. Mean and median together reveal whether a data set is skewed (a big gap between them suggests outliers pulling the mean). Standard deviation reveals how spread out the values are around that center. Looking at the full set of measures together gives a much more complete picture than any one number alone.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does this calculator show both mean and median?

Comparing them reveals skew. If mean and median are close, the data is roughly symmetric. If they differ a lot, the data is skewed by outliers — median tends to be the more reliable 'typical value' in that case.

What's the difference between variance and standard deviation?

Variance is the average squared deviation from the mean, expressed in squared units (which can be hard to interpret directly). Standard deviation is the square root of variance, bringing the measure back into the original units of the data, which is why it's more commonly reported.

Can I use this for both a sample and a full population?

Yes — the calculator computes both population and sample versions of variance and standard deviation, since the correct formula depends on whether your data represents an entire population or a sample drawn from a larger one.
